## How the long-arc pool is built

1. The engine loads season-level stats from two CSVs:
   - `data/nflverse/player_stats_season.csv.gz` — 1999–2025 (canonical nflverse).
   - `data/nflverse/player_stats_season_pre1999.csv.gz` — 1980–1998 (built by `scripts/build_pre1999_corpus.py` which scrapes PFR season tables through the Wayback Machine).
2. Concatenates into `PlayerCareer` objects (keyed by `player_id`; gsis_id for 1999+, `pfr_*` for pre-1999).
3. Classifies long-arc by `is_long_arc()` in `src/dynasty/engine/similarity_v1.py` line 240:
   - retired by 2022, OR
   - 8+ NFL seasons, OR
   - last_age ≥ 33 with 6+ seasons.

The `dynasty.db` SQLite file has 12,171 player meta rows but **no draft-year data populated** and is not used as the engine corpus. The corpus is the two CSVs above.

The PFR draft-class scraper (`scripts/refresh_pfr_draft_classes.py`) only writes `data/pfr/draft_class_<YEAR>.json` files (currently 2022–2026) and is consumed by the **Prospects** tab to flag drafted college players. It does **not** feed the long-arc comp pool.

## Coverage gap

| Era       | In engine corpus? | Notes                                                                 |
|-----------|-------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1936–1959 | **No**            | Pre-modern era. Single-platoon. Skill-pos boundaries fuzzy.           |
| 1960–1969 | **No**            | AFL existed; PFR has both leagues stitched. Significant offense data. |
| 1970–1979 | **No**            | Modern post-merger era. Real fantasy-relevant skill players.          |
| 1980–1998 | Yes (pre-1999 csv)| 1,355 distinct players, ~257 skill-player-seasons/yr.                 |
| 1999–2025 | Yes (nflverse)    | 11,353 distinct players, ~545 skill-player-seasons/yr.                |

Why ~257 vs ~545 per year for older vs newer? Two reasons:
1. Fewer NFL teams pre-1980 → fewer seasonal roster slots.
2. Pre-1999 only counts skill positions (QB/RB/WR/TE); nflverse has every offensive position.

Extending the pre-1999 scraper from 1980 → 1936 gives 44 additional draft years. Conservatively assuming ~150 skill-player-seasons/year averaged across that span (older years are smaller leagues with 8–14 teams), we expect to add roughly:

- **~3,000–5,000 player-seasons** (1936–1979)
- **~1,200–1,800 distinct new players**
- **Long-arc pool growth: 2,440 → ~3,500–4,000** (every additional player gets `last_season ≤ 1979` so they auto-qualify by the retired-through filter)

## What Phil's "pull every drafted player" implies

Two interpretations:
1. **Maximal:** Pull every player from PFR's `/draft/{year}.htm` pages for 1936–2025 (~22,500 drafted humans) and register them in `dynasty.db` as bio rows, even if they never produced a fantasy-relevant season.
2. **Engine-pragmatic:** Pull every player who *produced* an NFL season meeting the skill-position universe threshold from 1936–2025. This is what actually moves the long-arc comp pool.

Phil's stated goal is "more dynamic active-player rankings", which is driven by the **comp pool size**, which only includes players with actual seasonal production. So interpretation (2) is what's actionable.

We will **also** extend the PFR draft-class scraper to cover 1936–2026 (was 2022–2026) so the Prospects tab and any downstream code that joins on draft year has the full universe. That's cheap once we already have the PFR scraping infrastructure warmed up.

## Risks / mitigations

- **PFR pre-1960 schema drift:** Early PFR pages may not have all four tables (`fantasy`, `passing`, `rushing`, `receiving`). The parser already skips missing tables (logs warning, returns `[]`). Worst case the very early seasons contribute few players, which is fine — we still get them.
- **Position normalization:** Pre-1970 "halfback" / "fullback" / "flanker" / "split end" tokens already mapped in `POSITION_MAP` (RB, FB → RB; FL, SE → WR). Single-platoon era is the real issue — some players were both QB and DB. The skill-position filter handles that.
- **Universe threshold mismatch:** `_qualifies()` uses `carries ≥ 50 OR targets ≥ 20 OR receptions ≥ 15 OR attempts ≥ 100`. These thresholds are calibrated for the 16-game modern era; in the 12-game / 14-game eras they may be slightly lenient. We'll keep them as-is for v3.9 since the engine's downstream career-length filter (8+ seasons or retired) further selects.
- **No targets pre-1992:** Already handled by the `receptions ≥ 15` fallback in `_qualifies()`.
- **Wayback rate limits:** The existing 4s throttle worked for 1980-1998. We'll keep it. 176 additional fetches at 4s each = 12 min.
- **Era-pace table:** The engine builds an `EraPaceTable` from the corpus. Adding pre-1980 seasons may create a 5th era. Need to check `era_for_season()` and `ERA_BOUNDS` to confirm older seasons don't break the era logic.
